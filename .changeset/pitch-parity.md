Both players get the same pitch, and it fills the phone. The person who created a match
had the invite — a heading, a share button, the raw link and a paragraph — stacked above
the board, and the board is what gives way: 61% of the width against their opponent's 90%.
It stayed on screen after the opponent had joined, because the decision was made from the
row the lobby had read rather than the live one.

The invite is now a sheet over the screen, gone the moment the seat is taken, and
re-openable from "More" while it is not. The turn banner folded into the scoreboard line
that already said the same thing. An online pitch is now the same size as a hotseat one.

Alongside: the header says "online · vs <name>" rather than "hotseat", and the scoreboard
is written from the reader's side — "you" attacking up, "opponent" down — so it agrees with
the board each player is looking at. See ADR 0036.
